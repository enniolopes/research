# Missingness as a target and identification problem

Missingness is not only a preprocessing choice. It can change who is represented, what quantity is estimated and which assumptions connect the observed sample to the target population.

Before choosing a missing-data procedure, record:

- which variables are missing and at what unit/time level;
- the amount and pattern of missingness;
- whether missingness changes inclusion in the analytic population;
- plausible mechanisms relating missingness to observed and unobserved quantities;
- the estimand under the proposed handling;
- diagnostics or design evidence available for those assumptions;
- sensitivity analysis for material departures.

Complete-case analysis, imputation, weighting or model-based handling are candidates, not defaults. If alternatives target different populations or require materially different unverifiable assumptions, the choice is substantive and returns to the protocol/human gate.
