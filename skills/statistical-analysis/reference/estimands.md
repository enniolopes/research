# Estimands first

An estimator is not the scientific target. Before choosing a method, write the quantity the study is trying to learn.

A usable estimand states, as applicable: target population; unit; exposure/intervention or contrast; outcome; time horizon; intercurrent-event handling; and summary measure. For associational work, state the conditional or marginal contrast and the population over which it is summarized. For causal-leaning work, state the intervention/contrast and the identification assumptions separately from the estimand itself.

Two candidate analyses that target different populations, contrasts, outcomes, time points or summaries are not competing models for one question. They answer different questions and require a protocol decision.

Before an estimator is admitted, answer:

1. What quantity would this estimator converge to under its assumptions?
2. Is that quantity the recorded estimand?
3. Which design/identification assumptions connect observed data to it?
4. What dependence structure affects inference?
5. What observation would make the planned interpretation invalid or narrower?

If these cannot be answered, the method is a candidate only. Do not fit it as the confirmatory primary test.
