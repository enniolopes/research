# Dependence before inference

Intervals and tests depend on how observations share information. Before defining inferential uncertainty, identify the unit that can induce dependence: repeated measurements, clusters, geography, networks, time or shared shocks.

Do not map a dependence label directly to one estimator. Instead record:

- the substantive source of dependence;
- the level(s) at which observations may co-move;
- whether the number of independent units is adequate for the planned inferential procedure;
- the planned diagnostic or design argument;
- the inferential method and why it respects that structure;
- the predeclared fallback if the diagnostic/design condition fails.

A method remembered as conventional is not enough. Method-specific rules belong in source-read method cards. Until then, narrowing the claim or returning `NOT_VERIFIED` is preferable to inventing precision.
