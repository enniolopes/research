# Inference and local revision

Read when a claim interprets a measure, attributes a mechanism, compares explanations, or must be revised after evidence changes. Store the work in the existing protocol/analysis plan, manuscript inference and decision log; do not create a parallel knowledge ledger.

## Make the inferential bridge inspectable

Before adopting an interpretation, distinguish the observation from what it is said to mean. In the claim's interpretation boundary or nearby manuscript reasoning, record only the links material to this claim:

- construct and operational measure, with the evidence and conditions allowing that interpretation;
- target population, context and predicted observable consequence;
- a plausible rival that could produce the same result, and an observation or comparison where predictions differ;
- what the current design can distinguish, what remains unidentified, and the resulting permitted wording.

For construct-dependent work, a small table of construct → measure → prediction → rival → discriminating observation is a usable nomological representation. It records a theory's commitments, not established truth. Do not require a network for a directly operational descriptive claim, or invent a rival just to fill a field. Record a missing discriminator rather than pretending the available data identify one explanation.

Before attributing a gain to a proposed mechanism, compare with a credible simpler explanation under comparable information, resources and evaluation. A privileged diagnostic baseline can locate an explanation without demonstrating a deployable alternative. Preserve that distinction. Judge a representation by its contribution to the question, not by its philosophical vocabulary.

Do not count multiple papers, reviewers or measurements as independent support without examining shared data, sources and assumptions. A source can be reliable for one proposition and inadequate for another. Criticism is a proposal to inspect, not an instruction to change the conclusion.

## Choose the next useful check

Within the declared budget, prefer the feasible observation that could change the consequential inference over repeated checks of already settled details. State the rival predictions, approximate cost and decision consequence. If no available check can distinguish them, narrow the claim or retain uncertainty. Do not invent numerical information-gain estimates, require endless objections, or block work whose relevant conditions are already satisfied.

## Revise only what the correction warrants

1. Identify the changed source/data/assumption/result and retain its previous identity/version. Corrected computed results receive a new run/result identity under the existing run contract.
2. Use graph `changed` and source inspection to find candidate consequences. The graph is incomplete navigation, not proof that an unlisted claim is unaffected; check manuscript, figures, summaries and shared assumptions too.
3. For each affected claim inspect the actual inference and any remaining independent support. Record whether its scope/support is retained, narrowed, withdrawn or pending reassessment. These are revision dispositions, not new hypothesis terminal states. Loss of one support does not prove falsity; do not withdraw an unaffected empirical result merely because its preferred explanation failed.
4. Append a decision naming the changed evidence, affected claim IDs, old/new evaluation, surviving support, unresolved checks and revision condition. If superseding a decision, name it explicitly. Update the authoritative manuscript/plan and map pointers; preserve history. A deferred reassessment must remain visible and cannot silently pass CLAIM/PUBLISH.
5. Rerun only affected computations/checks and obtain fresh review of material changes. Unrelated supported work can proceed.

The scientific adequacy of these links requires judgment. A parser can check identity or reachability, not establish construct validity, causal identification or truth.
